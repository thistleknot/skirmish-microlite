# Inspirations and borrowed rules

This ledger names the games and rules sources that shaped Microlite Tactics. It distinguishes rules used in play from design influence and sources that were reviewed without a specific current rule being traced to them. A familiar mechanic is not attributed to a source unless the game's notes identify that relationship.

## Toolkit for design

The design intent for advancement, feats, and the campaign's growth is in [`intent.md`](./intent.md). The sources below are a toolkit to draw from when realizing that intent; they are not rules the game must copy.

## Rules borrowed or adapted

### Microlite20

**Role: rules foundation and adapted reference.** The game uses Microlite20's d20, attributes, hit points, armor class, skills, spell framework, and equipment-price vocabulary. Spell costs, spell DCs, signature-spell discounts, and several spell effects were checked against the 2020 collection. Microlite20 has one thing per turn; this game adds movement plus one action. Its checkpoint advancement, four-person teams, HP-paid spell debt, campaign economy, and feats are house rules. The collection contains multiple variants, so it is a reference rather than a wholesale rules import.

### Basic Fantasy RPG, rules as written in the r97 Lite reference

**Role: adapted combat rules.** The game uses natural 1 as a miss, rear-attack bonuses, parting attacks, monster morale and fleeing, and the cleric-versus-undead table. Holy water and oil damage ideas also come from this source. The game's grenade range, miss behavior, prices, morale values, and action costs are game-specific where they differ. Basic Fantasy's flat cover penalty was considered, then replaced by line of sight: blocking terrain prevents a shot instead of granting a numeric cover bonus.

### Pathfinder LITE (Tablet edition)

**Role: adapted maneuvers, charge, and climbing checks.** Trip, bull rush, and Aid Another use opposed CMB-style checks; Charge uses a straight, clear approach ending beside the target; and +2 ledges use the Physical + STR Climb check form. This game makes maneuvers consume the full activation, limits charge to heroes, and sets its own check DCs and movement rules. Natural 1 misses is shared with Basic Fantasy and is credited to both in the code notes.

### HeroQuest

**Role: adapted dungeon procedures and traps.** HeroQuest informs line-of-sight blocking, doors that stay open once crossed, room-based searching, pit traps, and spear traps. This game uses hexes and its own search radius; monsters can open doors; sprung pits remain open; and shields deflect spears. These details are adaptations, not a complete HeroQuest rules port.

### Moldvay Basic (B/X D&D)

**Role: adapted wandering-monster check.** The game rolls Moldvay's 1-in-6 wandering check, adapted to search rounds: after every second completed search round, a 1 on 1d6 brings 1d3 wanderers from the floor's band, placed out of sight at about 2d6 x 2 hexes. The game does not copy Moldvay's encounter tables, distances or turn structure. Moldvay's chest treasure, which scales by dungeon level, informed the per-floor chest scaling, adapted to a straight ×floor scale rather than Moldvay's 10x steps.

### Microlite20 RPG Collection (2020 edition)

**Role: rules reference.** The collection was consulted for rest, skills, spells, equipment, and progression concepts. Where the collection's variants differ, the game's explicit contract and house rules control. No claim is made that every edition in the collection was adopted.

## Campaign and tactical design influences

### Mordheim

**Role: campaign inspiration.** Mordheim's persistent warband, reserve turnover, post-battle recovery, equipment, and advancement informed the active roster, reserve roster, town services, and between-delve progression. The game does not copy Mordheim's tabletop campaign tables, injury system, or combat rules.

### D&D Rules Cyclopedia

**Role: campaign inspiration.** Its optional teacher-led training, skills and weapon mastery, and retainers/hirelings informed the town teacher and reserve-roster ideas. The campaign uses its own checkpoint choices and team rules; no Rules Cyclopedia table is copied. The reviewed PDF had parsing problems, so exact table-level attribution is not claimed.

### Age of Fantasy: Skirmish, “One Page Skirmish” v3.4.1

**Role: interface inspiration.** Its compact action choices and clear objectives informed presentation and objective feedback. Its alternating army activation, multi-model wounds, and army morale were not transplanted into this individual-hero RPG.

### UniWar

**Role: tactical design inspiration.** The game's movement previews, readable objectives, terrain choices, recovery point, and directional positioning were explored as ideas for this hex tactics game. The resulting altar, shrine, path preview, weighted terrain, and coordination rules are implemented as this game's own rules; this ledger does not claim a one-to-one UniWar rules import.

### Final Fantasy Tactics

**Role: turn-structure inspiration.** Move followed by an action informs the game's hero activation flow. Microlite20's one-thing-per-turn structure is deliberately expanded to support this pattern.

### Chainmail

**Role: terrain reference.** Chainmail's hills and ditch/rampart movement limits, and its firm-support firing modifier in gunpowder rules, informed terrain discussion. The game's +1 ranged attack per z-level and weapon-specific vertical reach are house rules, not Chainmail rules.

### NetHack

**Role: feature-design reference.** The official [NetHack repository](https://github.com/NetHack/NetHack) was cloned and inspected at release `NetHack-5.0.0_Released` (`16ff59115315917b93185d026aeefea06db9b0f4`). Its feature families are inventoried in [`features.md`](./features.md). NetHack has not supplied a rule currently implemented in Microlite Tactics; recommendations drawn from its features are explicitly marked as design proposals. The stairs direction convention (down, and way out) follows NetHack's down and up stairs; the stair drawing is original art, and the direction is a display convention, not a rule.

## Reviewed, with no specific current rule attributed

### Dead Simple Fantasy RPG Rules, 4th edition

Its aptitude + training + profession approach to training was reviewed. The current campaign instead uses its own skill ranks and teacher-gated checkpoint choices; no specific mechanic is attributed to this source as implemented.
